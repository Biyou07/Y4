1. Experimental Results & Step Analysis
Step 1 — Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL) Formula
We implement Friis' free-space path loss equation for isotropic antennas (Gt = Gr = 1): PLFS (dB) = 20 log10(d) + 20 log10(f) + 32.44, where d is distance in km and f is frequency in MHz. We simulate f = 2400 MHz (2.4 GHz Wi-Fi) from 10 m (0.01 km) to 10 km.

Step 3 — Log-Distance Path Loss Model
To model real-world obstacles, reflections, and scattering, we simulate the empirical Log-Distance model at f = 2400 MHz with reference distance d0 = 0.1 km (100 m, PL(d0) = 80.04 dB): PL(d) = PL(d0) + 10n log10(d / d0) for n ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5}.

2. Answers to Discussion Questions

Question 1: Why does higher frequency lead to greater path loss?

Answer: As carrier frequency f increases, the wavelength λ = c / f becomes shorter. Because an isotropic receiving antenna's effective capture area is proportional to λ2 (Ae = λ2 / 4π), a higher-frequency antenna physically intercepts a smaller portion of the expanding spherical wavefront. Mathematically, PLFS includes +20 log10(f), so doubling the frequency adds +6.02 dB of path loss at the same distance (e.g., +8.52 dB at 2.4 GHz and +16.18 dB at 5.8 GHz compared to 900 MHz).

Question 2: What environmental factors can increase the path loss exponent n?

Answer: In free space (n=2), waves spread without obstacles. In real environments, n increases to 3–5+ due to:
• Physical obstructions: Concrete/brick walls, floors, furniture, metal structures, and buildings that absorb or block signals.
• Foliage & terrain: Trees, wet leaves, hills, and uneven ground.
• Multipath & scattering: Reflections off buildings, vehicles, or indoor surfaces causing destructive interference, plus diffraction around sharp corners.
• Clutter & atmospheric density: Crowded indoor spaces, dense urban canyons, and rain/humidity at higher frequencies.

Question 3: Why do indoor Wi-Fi networks have smaller coverage areas than outdoor cellular networks?

Answer: Three main factors explain this difference:
• Higher path loss exponent (n): Indoor walls and furniture push n to 4–5, whereas outdoor cellular links typically see n ≈ 3 (or 2 in open areas). Our simulation showed range dropping from 996.6 m (n=3) to 560.9 m (n=4) and 397.3 m (n=5).
• Higher carrier frequencies: Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz / 5.8 GHz) suffers higher free-space loss and weaker wall penetration than lower cellular bands (700–900 MHz).
• Lower transmit power & antenna height: Wi-Fi routers are capped at low power (∼+20 dBm) indoors, while cellular base stations transmit at much higher power (+40 to +46 dBm) from tall towers with clearer line-of-sight.

Question 4: How do transmit power and receiver sensitivity influence coverage distance?

Answer: Together, they determine the maximum allowed path loss: PLmax = Pt - Prx,min. Increasing transmit power Pt or improving (lowering) receiver sensitivity Prx,min increases PLmax and extends coverage distance. Because path loss grows logarithmically as 10n log10(d / d0), every +10n dB gain in the link budget multiplies the maximum coverage distance by 10× (or doubles the distance for every +6 dB in free space n=2, and every +12 dB indoors n=4).
