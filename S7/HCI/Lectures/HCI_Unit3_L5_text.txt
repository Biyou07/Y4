3. Persona
in UCD

A persona is the
customer your
business can
finally talk to
before spending
money on the
product.

●

3.1. Definition
and Purpose

●
●

A
persona
is
a
fictional
yet
research-grounded archetype that
represents a cluster of users sharing
similar goals, behaviors, and contexts
of use
It is neither a statistical average, nor a
real individual, nor a market segment.
Functions: fostering empathy, creating
a shared understanding within the
design team, and supporting the
justification of design decisions.

3.1. Definition
and Purpose

●

Design for one: satisfying one specific
persona
fully
is
better
than
half-satisfying everyone (after Cooper,
2004)

3.2. Anatomy of a
Persona

●
●
●
●
●
●
●

Identity: name, photograph, age, brief
biography
Goals: the outcomes the user seeks to
achieve
Tasks: the activities performed with the
system
Pain points: frustrations and barriers
Technological profile: devices, skills, usage
frequency
Context of use: physical, social, linguistic,
and network conditions
Representative quote: a synthesis of the
persona’s attitude

3.3. Typology of
Personas

1. Proto-persona
Constructed

from

the

team’s

assumptions; efficient but requires
empirical validation.

2. Research-based persona
Derived from empirical data (Real Data:
Interviews, Observation, Analytics).

3. Primary and secondary personas
● The primary persona determines the core
design;
● Secondary personas introduce additional
needs that do not conflict with the primary.

4. Negative persona

● An archetype of a user whose needs the product
deliberately are not addressed, or whose behavior
the design must prevent (e.g., a malicious actor).
●

It helps the team set clear boundaries on who the
system serves and avoid adding features that do
not benefit the primary personas.

4. Scenario

4.1. Definition
and structure

●

A scenario is a narrative describing
how a persona pursues a goal in a
specific context.

●

It has five elements: the persona
(who), the context (where and when),
the goal (why), the sequence of
actions, and the outcome. Where the
persona

describes

the

user,

scenario puts that user into action.

the

4.2. Worked
example

Samira, 62 years old, a Doctor
She receives her electricity bill in the evening and wants
to pay it from home to avoid the post office queue.
Usually her son Amine, who lives in another city, helps
her by phone, but this time she wants to learn to do it
herself. She hesitates before confirming because she
fears an irreversible error, and her connection drops
during the payment. She needs to know whether the
payment succeeded and to keep a receipt.

Requirements Derived from the
Scenario:
●
●
●
●

A guided flow that supports learning
without assistance.
An explicit confirmation step before
payment.
A visible payment status with recovery
after connection loss.
A downloadable receipt.
