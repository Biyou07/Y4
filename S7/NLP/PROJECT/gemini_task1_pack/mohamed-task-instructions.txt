Task 1 – Glossary v1 (Mon 9 Nov)
What to do: turn slang-glossary-v0.xlsx into a Google Sheet and clean it.
One row per term and per drug: split rows that mix substances (for example the tramadol row).
Write the canonical spelling in Arabic script and in Arabizi following darija-spelling-rules (ق for q/g; q, not 9, in Arabizi), and put the other spellings in variants.
Fix the script column.
Remove prices, dealer code words and adulteration details.
Add ambiguous (yes | no) and normal_meaning (what the word means outside drugs), and verified (yes | no): yes only if a written source or a second speaker confirms it.
Outcome
slang-glossary-v1 (Google Sheet) in 04-glossary-and-rules, columns: term_id | term | canonical_arabic | canonical_arabizi | variants | script | drug | ambiguous | normal_meaning | verified | source | notes.
Example row: G-012 | zatla | زطلة | zatla | zetla; ztla | arabizi | cannabis | no | – | yes | press article | common word for hashish.
A summary tab: number of terms per drug, ambiguous terms, verified terms.
Why it matters: the glossary drives the filters that found the real messages, the normaliser, and the drug detection in triage.
Task 2 – Context set (Fri 13 Nov)
What to do: this answers the meeting point that a word may or may not mean a drug depending on the sentence. For every ambiguous term (about 40):
at least 3 sentences where it means the drug and 2 where it does not;
take real sentences from messages_real_souha and messages_real_malak first (write their msg_id in origin), and invent the rest (origin = invented);
mix the scripts (Arabic, Arabizi, French mixing).
Outcome
context-set-v1 in 04-glossary-and-rules, about 200 rows, columns of section 4 of dataset-format-v1, at least 40% real sentences.
Example rows: C-031 | (term) | (canonical) | (sentence where it means the drug) | arabizi | drug | cannabis | M-S25-0042 | Mohamed; C-032 | same term | (sentence with its normal meaning) | arabic | not-drug | – | invented | Mohamed.
Why it matters: it teaches and tests the app to understand the word from its context instead of reacting to the word alone, so a normal sentence is not treated as drug talk and drug talk is not missed.
