# Project 16: A Conversational Agent Application Using the Algerian Dialect to Influence Community-Level Responses to Drug Addiction

## 1. Introduction and Objective

Drug consumption leads each year to the loss of thousands of human lives and generates considerable health, social, and economic costs. The high level of use observed in adulthood is largely explained by early initiation into these substances, followed by a gradual progression toward more severe forms of dependence. This reality makes drug addiction a major public health concern, requiring coordinated, multisectoral, and priority responses.

Drug use during adolescence constitutes a major risk factor, one that can durably impair the physical, psychological, and social development of young people. To curb this phenomenon, it is essential to implement early information, education, and prevention initiatives—starting from early childhood—and to strengthen them throughout the educational and community trajectory.

The most effective strategies rely on the development of psychosocial skills among children and parents, the promotion of protective behaviours, and, where necessary, the application of appropriate regulatory and community-based measures.

From a biological standpoint, drug consumption—particularly through intravenous routes—greatly increases the risk of transmitting HIV and other infections, such as hepatitis B and C, due to the sharing of contaminated injection equipment. Repeated drug use also undermines both mental and physical health: it can induce severe psychiatric disorders (such as schizophrenia, depression, and paranoia) and weaken the immune system, making users more vulnerable to opportunistic infections such as tuberculosis or certain cancers—especially in cases of HIV co-infection.

Injecting drugs with shared equipment (syringes, needles, but also spoons, filters, tourniquets, or straws used for sniffing) remains one of the main modes of transmission for HIV and viral hepatitis. It is important to recall that sharing preparation equipment—even in the absence of syringe sharing—is a common and high-risk practice, particularly for the transmission of hepatitis C, a danger still largely unrecognized among users.

This project aims to develop an LLM-powered application capable of interacting with users in Algerian dialect, thus bridging the communication gap and providing culturally relevant, easily understandable support. The solution will support individuals with substance use disorders in understanding the health risks associated with drug consumption, exploring evidence-based cessation strategies, and engaging with motivational prompts that encourage gradual reduction and eventual abstinence. By leveraging conversational AI, the project seeks to provide an accessible, trustworthy, and user-friendly assistant that reduces reliance on overburdened health services and increases the effectiveness of drug use reduction interventions.

---

## 2. Project Description

The project will combine data collection, LLM-based model adaptation, and optimisation for deployment on mobile devices or lightweight web platforms. Its core will be a multifunctional support environment tailored to drug takers, offering evidence-based educational content, personalized prompts, and simulated counselling dialogues. Importantly, the system will also account for the cultural and linguistic context of Algeria, ensuring advice feels relevant and engaging for the target group.

---

## 3. Core Components

### A. Data Collection and Curation
* **Collect and curate a dataset including:**
  * Health-related resources in Arabic and French about drug consumption effects and cessation.
  * Drug addiction campaign materials and existing awareness content from INSP (National Institute of Public Health) and other Algerian health authorities.
  * User-generated Q&A samples and anonymized counselling transcripts (where ethically available).
* **Adaptation:** Translate, adapt, and align these resources into Algerian dialect for natural conversational use.

### B. LLM-Based Model Training and Fine-Tuning
* Evaluate several open/free LLMs for their performance on Arabic dialect comprehension and generation.
* Fine-tune the most promising model with Algerian dialect corpora and drug-consumption-related data.
* Optimize the model for lightweight deployment on limited infrastructures (smartphones, rural internet).

### C. Application Features
* **Interactive Counselling Assistant:** Provides motivational support, answers common questions (e.g., cravings, withdrawal symptoms, alternative habits), and suggests practical strategies.
* **Personalized Goal Setting:** Allows users to set goals for reducing drug use gradually (aiming at total cessation) and tracks their progress.
* **Educational Modules:** Short, engaging explanations (text and optionally audio) of health risks, financial impacts, and social consequences of drug consumption.
* **Gamified Rewards System (Optional):** Encourages progress through badges, streaks, or small challenges.
* **Emergency Support Links:** Quick access to hotlines or local clinics for high-risk cases.

### D. Safety, Security, and Trustworthiness
* Ensure that sensitive user inputs (e.g., health conditions, personal struggles) are processed securely.
* Establish fact-checking pipelines to guarantee that generated content is medically accurate and aligned with WHO and Algerian Ministry of Health guidelines.
* Implement safeguards to filter harmful or biased outputs.
* Provide disclaimers clarifying the assistant is not a substitute for professional medical advice.

---

## 4. System Evaluation

Evaluation metrics will include:
* **Linguistic Quality:** Coherence, clarity, and naturalness of Algerian dialect responses.
* **Behavioural Impact:** Effectiveness in reducing drug use habits (self-reported surveys, pilot studies).
* **Usability:** User satisfaction with interface simplicity and accessibility on limited devices.
* **Trustworthiness:** Accuracy and reliability of health-related outputs (no hallucinations, safety, etc.).

---

## 5. Expected Outcomes

* A mobile/web-based assistant capable of interacting in Algerian dialect to provide drug addiction reduction support.
* A curated dataset of Algerian dialect health-related conversations on drug consumption.
* Technical documentation and a research paper analysing the use of LLMs for public health interventions in North Africa.

---

## 6. Expected Impact

This project is expected to improve public health outcomes by directly addressing one of Algeria’s most persistent behavioural health challenges: drug addiction. By offering culturally relevant, dialect-based, and accessible support, the system can empower individuals to make healthier choices, reduce drug addiction prevalence, and decrease long-term healthcare costs. Furthermore, the platform can serve as a blueprint for similar health-focused dialect-based interventions in Algeria and across the Arab world.
